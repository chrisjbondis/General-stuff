HRV & Resonance Breathing

A working reference for daily practice and sleep.

The core technique: resonance breathing

Slow, exhale-dominant breathing shifts the body toward parasympathetic (vagal) dominance. The strongest, most consistent effects show up at or below 6 breaths per minute — the "resonance frequency" for most adults, where breathing rhythm and natural heart-rate oscillations sync up.

Dawn
5s in / 5s out
6 breaths/min — dead center of the resonance range. Daytime baseline / general regulation.
Dusk
4s in / 7s out
Longer exhale bias. Evening wind-down, targets sleep onset specifically.
Quick reset
4s in / 6s out
Shorter sessions, still exhale-dominant. For a mid-day reset.

Why plain 5-in/5-out, no hold

A hybrid pattern like 5s in / 3s hold / 6s out was tested and works — the hold stimulates lung stretch receptors, adding a second (different) pathway to sustained vagal output alongside the exhale itself, and it can act as a useful attentional anchor. But it runs slightly under the resonance rate. Plain 5/5 lands almost exactly on 6 breaths/min and is the most-studied, most reliable option if you're picking one thing to do consistently.

Dosage: what to actually do

Weeks 1–2

5 minutes, twice a day. Consistency beats duration early on — a 10-minute session skipped half the time is worse than 5 minutes done reliably. The goal is to make it automatic before making it longer.

Once it's a habit

Build to 10 minutes, twice a day. This is the dose used in the clinical studies showing the strongest, most durable HRV improvements (sustained over 8–12 weeks). Jumping straight to 20 min/day from a standing start tends to backfire — it feels like a bigger ask and gets skipped on exactly the days it's needed most.

Extra sessions

No downside to additional 5-minute sessions through the day — benefits appear to compound with frequency, not just duration, and it doubles as a solid meditation. Only caution: if lightheaded or dizzy, ease off the pace or shorten the session.

Sleep: a different toolkit

Sleep breathing isn't just "do the same thing for longer" — three distinct tools, each solving a different part of the problem.

1. Dusk (extended exhale) — main wind-down ritual

4s in / 7s out, 15–20 minutes, no hold. A study on insomniacs found 20 minutes of paced breathing before bed reduced time to fall asleep, night wakings, and total time spent awake. Run before bed, sitting or lying down, eyes closed if comfortable.

  • Minutes 1–2: still consciously counting
  • Minutes 3–5: counting fades, breath smooths out
  • Minutes 8–10+: heaviness, slower heart rate, pre-sleep drift

If breathing naturally slows past the count without effort, that's the exercise working — no need to force precision the whole way through.

2. 4-7-8 — the lights-out signal

4 in / hold 7 / 8 out, ~4 cycles (2 minutes). Not a long session — a short ritual right at the edge of sleep that signals to the brain "time to wind down." Works better as a nightly habit than as a rescue tool on an already-bad night.

3. Cyclic sighing — the 2:30am tool

Two inhales through the nose (one normal, one short "top-off"), then one long, slow exhale through the mouth. A Stanford study (Balban et al., 2023) found 5 minutes daily outperformed mindfulness meditation for lowering arousal and improving mood. Short and low-effort enough to use half-asleep — the specific tool for a middle-of-the-night wake-up, rather than lying there escalating.

Skip at night: box breathing

The 4-4-4-4 pattern (holds on both ends) is built for daytime alertness-under-pressure, not drowsiness. Save it for a stressful moment during the day.
